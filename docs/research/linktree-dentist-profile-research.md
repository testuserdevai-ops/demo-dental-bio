# Dentist Linktree profile research

Research date: 2026-08-11  
Product context: [Trello card #54 — Add theme and premium layout customization](https://trello.com/c/gnTNTcDF/54-add-theme-and-premium-layout-customization)

## Purpose

Identify the ordered content skeletons used by dentist and dental-practice Linktree profiles, then use the recurring structures to define useful DentalBio starter templates. This is an information-architecture study, not a recommendation to reproduce Linktree's visual design.

## Method

- Inspected 32 unique, publicly rendered Linktree profiles: the two supplied examples and 30 additional dentist, orthodontist, cosmetic-practice, and dental-clinic profiles.
- Recorded the visible order of identity, calls to action, service, location, proof, content, social, and commerce elements.
- Ignored Linktree-owned footer, discovery, reporting, privacy, and account-information content.
- Normalized differently worded elements into the taxonomy below while retaining the original visible labels in the profile inventory.
- Profiles are live and may change after the research date.

## Normalized section taxonomy

| Element             | Meaning in DentalBio                                                             |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hero                | Wide visual, campaign artwork, or prominent feature card before or near identity |
| Identity            | Profile photo/logo, practitioner or practice name, credentials, verification     |
| Positioning         | One-line bio, specialty, location summary, or differentiator                     |
| Social row          | Compact social/contact icons rather than full-width links                        |
| Primary CTA         | The dominant booking, consultation, assessment, or contact action                |
| Secondary CTA       | Website, WhatsApp, phone, virtual consult, or alternate conversion path          |
| Services            | Individual treatments, grouped services, or treatment-category links             |
| Locations/providers | Branch, practitioner, appointment-type, or provider routing                      |
| Proof               | Reviews, testimonials, before/after work, awards, or case gallery                |
| About/team          | Dentist biography, team, credentials, affiliations, or media coverage            |
| Practical details   | Address, hours, payment, financing, insurance, emergency information             |
| Resources           | Blog posts, FAQs, guides, downloads, newsletters, or educational videos          |
| Community           | Recruitment, events, campaigns, competitions, clubs, or membership               |
| Commerce            | Products, courses, books, affiliate collections, or paid downloads               |

## Cross-profile findings

### The recurring order grammar

Most effective profiles follow this sequence even when individual modules differ:

1. **Recognize** — visual identity, name, credentials, specialty.
2. **Understand** — one-line positioning or reassurance.
3. **Act** — one primary consultation, booking, assessment, or contact action.
4. **Choose** — treatments, locations, providers, or appointment modes.
5. **Trust** — reviews, testimonials, results, credentials, team, or press.
6. **Resolve friction** — payment, insurance, emergency information, address, hours.
7. **Learn or follow** — FAQs, articles, downloads, newsletter, and social links.

The weakest structures lose this grammar by mixing SEO links, social links, services, and unrelated products at the same visual priority.

### Universally useful modules

- Identity block: profile image/logo, name, specialty or role.
- Short positioning statement.
- One dominant primary CTA.
- Optional social/contact row that does not compete with the CTA.

### Common differentiators

- Location or provider routing for multi-site practices.
- Service grouping rather than a flat wall of treatments.
- Review/testimonial links close to the conversion action.
- Virtual assessment or smile-preview tools for orthodontic/cosmetic practices.
- Financing and payment information for higher-value treatments.
- Before/after work and visual feature cards for cosmetic dentistry.

### Specialized modules

- Emergency information.
- Recruitment and community campaigns.
- Professional speaking, education, courses, and affiliations.
- Product shops, affiliate collections, books, and paid resources.
- Newsletter, VIP club, and downloadable guide capture.

### Ordering lessons for DentalBio

1. Booking usually deserves the first full-width action after identity; social icons should not push it below the fold.
2. Long service catalogs need headings, progressive disclosure, or a dedicated service view.
3. Multi-location profiles should route by intent—location, provider, or appointment type—not display an unexplained list of branches.
4. Reviews work best as proof after the primary CTA, while “leave a review” is an owner/customer-retention action and should not dominate a new-patient funnel.
5. Cosmetic practices benefit from a large visual or transformation card, but the hero must not obscure the dentist's identity or CTA.
6. Content, press, and educational resources are valuable for authority, but belong below conversion and proof unless the profile's main audience is other professionals.
7. Commerce needs explicit audience separation so patient booking is not buried under products or courses.

## Product implications

- A **template** should define module selection, order, default labels, and emphasis.
- A **theme** should define tokens such as color, type, surface, button shape, spacing, and image treatment.
- A **premium layout variant** may change composition—for example, a wide hero with overlapping identity—but must preserve semantic reading order and the template's module order.
- Users should be able to switch themes without changing content order, and switch templates only after previewing the reordering.
- The starter flow should ask about practice type, conversion goal, number of locations/providers, proof assets, financing, and whether the audience includes other dentists.
- Every generated profile should begin with no more than one primary CTA and one secondary CTA above the first content section.

## Research limitations

- The sample is purposive rather than statistically representative; it was selected to expose distinct dentist-profile structures.
- Linktree pages can change at any time, and some profiles contain temporary campaigns or stale links.
- The study records visible information architecture, not conversion performance.
- Image presence and placement were inspected, but downloadable assets were not copied and visual styles are not intended for reproduction.
